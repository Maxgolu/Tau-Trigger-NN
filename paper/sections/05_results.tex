\subsection{What the results tell us}
\label{sec:analysis}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\Cref{fig:results_analysis} highlights two controlled comparisons. The supporting tables, uncertainties and broader evidence are collected in \cref{app:analysis_weighting,app:analysis_representation,app:analysis_capacity,app:analysis_loss}.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_results_analysis.pdf}
  \caption{(a) Remaining OR trade-off after inverse-frequency (IF) weighting: adding the comparator exchanges low-energy efficiency for plateau gains. Both sides use IF; the BCE-to-IF comparison is in \cref{tab:analysis}. (b) Effect of adding EM2 and $\Frac$, then removing the core convolution. Abbreviations: \cref{tab:naming}. Supporting comparisons: \cref{app:analysis_weighting,app:analysis_representation}.}
  \label{fig:results_analysis}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{(a) More weight on energetic taus can help.}
The results support the hypothesis in \cref{sec:loss}: emphasising energetic rather than soft taus often helped. Compared with BCE, IF reduced the selected TOB budget (\cref{tab:analysis}). \Cref{fig:results_analysis}a shows the resulting trade-off: after IF training, OR buys a small plateau gain at a larger low-energy cost, so NN-only can win globally. This is input-dependent, not a universal preference for IF or negative $p$ (\cref{app:analysis_weighting}).

\paragraph{(b) Spatial learning and engineered features complement each other.}
CNNs recovered some taus missed by the MLP, consistent with sensitivity to spatial shower patterns. The larger gain came from the fine-grained EM2 input; adding $\Frac$ remained useful, while removing the core convolution retained most performance with fewer parameters (\cref{app:analysis_representation}).

\paragraph{(c) More parameters were not the main route to improvement.}
Wider MLPs and deeper CNNs gave no systematic advantage in the tested families. Similar errors across core-only models point to limited input discrimination rather than insufficient capacity (\cref{app:analysis_capacity}).

\paragraph{(d) The objective controls the trade-off across energies.}
Constrained fine-tuning redistributed efficiency towards protected or rewarded regions, usually with a small global change. The best loss depended on the inputs, and the lowest-energy band remained difficult across methods (\cref{app:analysis_loss}).

\subsection{Final evaluation of the pair model}
\label{sec:pair_results}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Seeing two TOBs together improves the turn-on.}
The selected model scores every pair of finite-$\ptTOB$ TOBs and gives every
event equal weight during training. Unlike the fixed-rate comparisons above,
we did not recalibrate its thresholds on the test set. The checkpoint and both
branch thresholds were selected on validation and then applied unchanged; the
$\ptTOB$ baseline uses
$q_e\geq\SI{19.4}{GeV}$. The final test contains $64{,}286$ events:
$14{,}509$ signal and $49{,}777$ background events. Its $361{,}685$ finite
TOBs form $866{,}872$ unordered pairs; 589 events contain fewer than two finite
TOBs and therefore fail both branches. The selected epochs are 17, 18 and 20
for seeds 42, 123 and 456, respectively.

For this subsection only, signal efficiency is the fraction of signal events
that fire. Elsewhere in the paper it is the fraction of truth-$\tau$ objects
accepted, so the two percentages are not directly comparable. For the
energy-bin results, we use eligible signal events in which a pair among the
four highest-$\ptTOB$ TOBs has two positive training labels and two finite
truth momenta. The event is assigned the lower of those two momenta. These
values are therefore also not directly comparable with
\cref{tab:results_main,fig:results_main}.

Across the three models, mean signal efficiency rises from $34.30\%$ to
$46.70\%$, a mean gain of 12.40 percentage points with a standard deviation of
0.32 points across seeds
(\cref{tab:pair_final}). The gain is concentrated in the turn-on energy
ranges: the mean differences are 21.43 points for $10$--$25\GeV$ and
22.19 points for $25$--$60\GeV$, while the already saturated region above
$60\GeV$ gains 0.78 points. This establishes the performance of the selected
pair model; the final test alone does not show which design choice caused the
improvement.

\paragraph{The efficiency gain comes with a small rate excess.}
The three test event fake rates, $0.5183$--$0.5223\%$, are all above the
required $0.5\%$. Seed 42 also misses the adjusted high-energy significance
requirement ($p_{\mathrm{adj}}=0.05946$). Our final success criterion required
both conditions, so it is not met, even though every model improves the
overall efficiency.
The operating point should be recalibrated on a new calibration sample before
deployment; the test set must not be reused for that correction. A
repository-local identity audit found no overlap with the enumerated Phase-5
confirmation archives. Complete absence of every possible historical or
external test exposure cannot be proven, so the result should be interpreted
with that exposure uncertainty. Exact thresholds, hashes and uncertainty
procedures appear in \cref{app:pair_protocol}.
